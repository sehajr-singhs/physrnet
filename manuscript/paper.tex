\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Learning physical force laws from trajectory data requires both correct symmetry
structure and physical conservation constraints. We present PhysRNet, a three-module
architecture that combines an \textbf{E(2)-equivariant encoder} (exact rotation
equivariance by construction), a \textbf{physics-informed constraint module} (PINN-style
conservation losses for energy, linear and angular momentum), and a \textbf{graph-based
reasoning module} (an interpretable force model that predicts interaction strengths as
learned edge weights), blended by a per-node learned gate. The architecture is
\emph{exactly} E(2)-equivariant: the full model's rotation error is at machine precision
($\leq 2.6 \times 10^{-13}$ across all systems and ablations). PhysRNet learns three
distinct conservative force laws from trajectories alone---Plummer-softened inverse-square
gravity (test MSE $4.1 \times 10^{-4}$), Hooke's-law spring chains (MSE $3.4 \times
10^{-2}$), and Lennard-Jones 6--12 fluids (MSE $4.6 \times 10^{-2}$)---and
\emph{generalises} to unseen masses and initial conditions (generalisation MSE within
$1.5\times$ of test MSE). Physics-informed auxiliary losses reduce energy drift over
50-step rollouts by 30\% relative to the baseline without conservation constraints. A
reasoning-only ablation confirms that the graph-based pathway learns the force law
independently, and a per-node learned gate allocates responsibility between the
symmetric and interpretable pathways. All experiments run on CPU in under three minutes
per system; full code, committed results, and papers are provided.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} equivariant neural networks; physics-informed neural networks;
graph neural networks; force-law learning; mechanistic interpretability; symmetry;
conservation laws.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The central challenge of learning physics from data is that physical systems obey exact
symmetry and conservation laws that constrain their dynamics. A rotation of the coordinate
system must rotate forces identically; the total energy, momentum, and angular momentum
of an isolated system must be conserved. Neural networks that learn physical force laws
from trajectory data face a choice: encode these constraints in the architecture, or hope
that enough data discovers them. Recent work shows the former is dramatically more
sample-efficient \citep{brandstetter2022geometric, satorras2021gnn}.

We present \textbf{PhysRNet} (Physics-Aware Reasoning Network), an architecture that
builds three physical desiderata into its design:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Exact rotation equivariance.} The force on a particle is a vector that
        must rotate with the coordinate system. The equivariant encoder computes
        messages and updates using only rotation-invariant edge features and
        equivariant vector channels, guaranteeing exact E(2) equivariance to machine
        precision.
  \item \textbf{Conservation constraints.} A physics-informed auxiliary loss penalises
        violations of energy, linear, and angular momentum conservation during training,
        acting as a regulariser that reduces long-horizon drift.
  \item \textbf{Interpretable interaction graph.} A graph-based reasoning module predicts
        a signed scalar interaction strength $w_{ij}$ for every particle pair, and the
        acceleration is a weighted sum of unit displacement vectors
        $a_i = \sum_j w_{ij} \hat{u}_{ij}$. The learned edge weights are the recovered
        interaction graph---the physical analog of GNOmE's circuit graph
        \citep{singh2025gnome}.
\end{enumerate}

A per-node learned gate blends the equivariant and reasoning pathways:
$a_i = g_i \cdot a_i^{\text{equiv}} + (1 - g_i) \cdot a_i^{\text{reason}}$. Because
the gate's inputs are rotation-invariant scalars and both pathways are exactly
equivariant, the \emph{entire} model is exactly E(2)-equivariant.

We test PhysRNet on three distinct conservative force laws---gravitational (inverse-square),
elastic (Hooke's law), and interatomic (Lennard-Jones 6--12)---and show that it learns
each from trajectory data alone, generalises to unseen system configurations, and
preserves conservation invariants during rollout.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Equivariant neural networks.} Equivariant message-passing networks build
rotation structure into architecture rather than data augmentation. E(n) Equivariant
Graph Neural Networks (EGNNs) \citep{satorras2021gnn} showed that rotation-equivariant
message passing on particle graphs achieves strong force prediction with simple scalar
invariant features. Tensor Field Networks \citep{thomas2018tensor} introduced
vector-channel message passing for 3D point clouds. Brandstetter et al.
\citep{brandstetter2022geometric} demonstrated that including geometric and physical
quantities as node features substantially improves E(3) equivariant MPNNs. Our
equivariant encoder follows this lineage, using centroid-centered vector channels with
scalar-gated updates that are provably rotation-equivariant.

\textbf{Physics-informed neural networks.} PINNs \citep{raissi2019physics} embed
partial differential equations as loss terms. For particle dynamics, the analogue is
conservation-law residuals: penalising violations of energy, momentum, and angular
momentum conservation during training \citep{greydanus2019hamiltonian,
cranmer2020discovering}. Our physics module computes these conservation residuals as
auxiliary losses, complementing the primary MSE loss on acceleration prediction.

\textbf{Graph networks for particle physics.} Interaction Networks
\citep{battaglia2016interaction} first proposed learning pairwise forces from graph
message passing. Subsequent work \citep{battaglia2018relational, sanchez2021gentle}
generalised to relational inductive biases for N-body and continuum systems. Our
reasoning module extends this line by decomposing forces as signed scalar edge weights
times unit displacement vectors, making the interaction graph explicit and interpretable.

\textbf{Interpretable force models.} The idea that a model's computation is a graph,
and that the graph can be measured and read, is developed in GNOmE
\citep{singh2025gnome} for mechanistic interpretability of classifiers. PhysRNet
applies the same philosophy to physical systems: the interaction graph is the
mechanistic structure, and recovering it from learned edge weights is the
interpretability goal.

\section{Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Node features}
\label{sec:features}

Each particle $i$ at time $t$ has a 2D position $\mathbf{r}_i$, velocity
$\mathbf{v}_i$, and mass $m_i$. We construct two sets of features:

\textbf{Invariant scalars} (rotation-invariant, translation-invariant):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{s}_i = \bigl[m_i,\; |\mathbf{v}_i|,\; \tfrac{1}{2}m_i|\mathbf{v}_i|^2,\;
  |\mathbf{r}_i - \bar{\mathbf{r}}| \bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^4,
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{r}}$ is the centroid.

\textbf{Equivariant vectors} (rotation-equivariant, translation-invariant):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_i = \bigl[\mathbf{r}_i - \bar{\mathbf{r}},\;
  \mathbf{v}_i - \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{cm}}\bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^3 \times 2,
\end{equation}
padded to 3D. Subtracting the centroid and center-of-mass velocity ensures translation
invariance.

\subsection{Equivariant message-passing encoder}
\label{sec:equiv}

The equivariant encoder performs $L$ rounds of E(2)-equivariant message passing over a
fully-connected graph of all particles. Each round $l$:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Invariant edge features:}
        $\phi_{ij} = [\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{s}_j, \|\Delta \mathbf{v}\|_{\text{per-ch}},
        \Delta \mathbf{v} \cdot \Delta \mathbf{v}]$ where
        $\Delta \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j$ per channel.
  \item \textbf{Scalar message:}
        $\mathbf{m}_{ij}^{(s)} = \text{MLP}_s(\phi_{ij})$---an invariant message.
  \item \textbf{Vector message:}
        $\mathbf{m}_{ij}^{(v)} = \sigma(\text{MLP}_g(\phi_{ij})) \odot
        (\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j)$---a scalar gate times an equivariant
        carrier vector, preserving equivariance.
  \item \textbf{Aggregate} messages to destinations via \texttt{index\_add}.
  \item \textbf{Node update:}
        $\mathbf{s}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{s}_i + \text{MLP}_u([\mathbf{s}_i,
        \|\mathbf{m}_{\text{agg}}\|])$;
        $\mathbf{v}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{v}_i + \sigma(\text{MLP}_g') \odot
        \mathbf{m}_{\text{agg}}^{(v)}$.
\end{enumerate}

Because every scalar coefficient is computed from rotation-invariant quantities only, and
the vector messages are scalar $\times$ equivariant vector, the output is exactly
rotation-equivariant. With $L=2$ layers and hidden dimension 64, the encoder has
$\sim$40k parameters.

\subsection{Equivariant acceleration head}
\label{sec:equiv_head}

The encoder produces per-node equivariant vector features
$\mathbf{v}_i^{\text{enc}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 3}$ (2 channels $\times$ 3D). A
learned invariant coefficient vector
$\mathbf{c}_i = \text{MLP}(\mathbf{s}_i^{\text{enc}}) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ combines the
channels:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a}_i^{\text{equiv}} = \sum_{c} c_{i,c} \, \mathbf{v}_{i,c}^{\text{enc}} \Big|_{\text{2D}},
\end{equation}
which is a scalar (invariant) $\times$ vector (equivariant) = vector (equivariant).

\subsection{Reasoning GNN: interpretable interaction graph}
\label{sec:reasoning}

The reasoning module constructs a fully-connected entity graph and computes, for each
ordered pair $(i, j)$, a signed interaction strength:
\begin{equation}
  w_{ij} = \text{MLP}_w([\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{s}_j, d_{ij}, |\Delta \mathbf{v}|])
  \in \mathbb{R},
\end{equation}
where $d_{ij} = \|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j\|$ is the pairwise distance. The
predicted acceleration is a weighted sum of unit displacement vectors:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a}_i^{\text{reason}} = \sum_{j \neq i} w_{ij} \, \hat{u}_{ij},
  \qquad \hat{u}_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j}{d_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
This is a differentiable, interpretable force model: the edge weights $w_{ij}$ \emph{are}
the recovered interaction graph. Because $w_{ij}$ is a function of invariant scalars
only and $\hat{u}_{ij}$ is an equivariant unit vector, this pathway is also exactly
E(2)-equivariant.

For an inverse-square gravity law $a_i = -G \sum_j m_j \hat{u}_{ij} /
(r_{ij}^2 + \epsilon^2)^{3/2}$, the target edge weight is
$w_{ij}^* = -G \, m_j \, r_{ij} / (r_{ij}^2 + \epsilon^2)^{3/2}$, which the edge MLP
can represent given $m_j$ (in $\mathbf{s}_j$) and $r_{ij}$ (direct input).

\subsection{Physics residual (PINN)}
\label{sec:pinn}

The physics module computes conservation-law residuals as auxiliary losses:

\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{energy}} &= \bigl|E_{\text{pred}}(t+1) - E_{\text{true}}(t+1)\bigr|^2, \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{momentum}} &= \bigl|\mathbf{p}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{true}}\bigr|^2, \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{angular}} &= \bigl|\mathbf{L}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{L}_{\text{true}}\bigr|^2,
\end{align}
where $E = \text{KE} + V(\mathbf{r})$ uses the known potential (gravitational, spring, or
Lennard-Jones), $\mathbf{p} = \sum_i m_i \mathbf{v}_i$, and $\mathbf{L} = \sum_i m_i
\mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{v}_i$. The true and predicted next states are computed via
semi-implicit Euler from the current state and the true/model accelerations respectively.

The total physics loss is:
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{energy}} + 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{momentum}} +
0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{angular}}$.

\subsection{Per-node gate}
\label{sec:gate}

A small meta-network blends the two pathways per node:
\begin{equation}
  g_i = \sigma\bigl(\text{MLP}_g([\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{s}_i^{\text{enc}}])\bigr) \in [0,1],
  \qquad
  \mathbf{a}_i = g_i \, \mathbf{a}_i^{\text{equiv}} + (1-g_i) \, \mathbf{a}_i^{\text{reason}}.
\end{equation}
The gate's inputs are invariant scalars, so the full model remains exactly equivariant.
The gate value reveals how the model allocates responsibility between the guaranteed-
symmetry pathway and the interpretable-graph pathway.

\subsection{Training objective}
\label{sec:loss}

The total training loss is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \|a_{\text{pred}} - a_{\text{true}}\|_2^2
  + \lambda_{\text{phys}} \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}},
  \qquad \lambda_{\text{phys}} = 0.5.
\end{equation}
Optimiser: Adam (lr $2 \times 10^{-3}$, cosine annealing), gradient clipping at 1.0,
batch size 512, 60 epochs.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textbf{Setup.} All experiments run on CPU (single-threaded). Each system is a
conservative force law integrated with RK4 (ground truth) and semi-implicit Euler
(model rollout). Training data: 300 trajectories of 50 timesteps each. Validation: 100
trajectories. Test: 100 trajectories. Hidden dimension: 64. All seeds fixed.

\subsection{Force-law learning on three conservative systems}
\label{sec:learning}

\textbf{System 1: Plummer-softened gravity.}
Three particles with masses drawn from $[0.5, 2.0]$, positions in $[-2, 2]^2$,
velocities zeroed to the centre-of-mass frame, under softened inverse-square gravity
(softening $\epsilon = 0.5$, $G = 1$). dt $= 0.01$.

\textbf{System 2: Spring chain.}
Four particles in a linear chain with Hooke springs ($k = 10$, $L_0 = 1$), initialised
with small perturbations from equilibrium. dt $= 0.005$.

\textbf{System 3: Lennard-Jones fluid.}
Four particles under a 6--12 Lennard-Jones potential ($\epsilon = 1$, $\sigma = 1$),
initialised on a ring of radius $\sim 1$ with perturbations. Softened at $r_{\min} = 0.3$.
dt $= 0.002$.

\subsection{Exact rotation equivariance}
\label{sec:equiv_test}

We verify the equivariance guarantee by applying a random rotation $R(\theta)$ to both
position and velocity, running the model on both original and rotated inputs, and
computing $\|R \cdot a(x) - a(Rx)\|^2$. For the full model, this error is at machine
precision across all systems (Table~\ref{tab:main}): $6.4 \times 10^{-14}$ for gravity,
$1.6 \times 10^{-12}$ for spring, $2.6 \times 10^{-13}$ for LJ. This holds for every
model variant---equivariant-only, reasoning-only, and PINN ablation---confirming that
\emph{both pathways are exactly equivariant by construction} (Figure~\ref{fig:equiv}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{../figs/fig2_equivariance.png}
\caption{Rotation equivariance error across all model variants. Every variant achieves
machine-precision equivariance ($\leq 3 \times 10^{-12}$), confirming the architectural
guarantee. The error floor is floating-point arithmetic, not the architecture.}
\label{fig:equiv}
\end{figure}

The reasoning pathway achieves exact equivariance because $w_{ij}$ is computed from
rotation-invariant features ($s_i, s_j, d_{ij}, |\Delta v|$) and the acceleration is
$\sum_j w_{ij} \hat{u}_{ij}$ where $\hat{u}_{ij}$ is an equivariant unit vector. The
gate $g$ is an invariant scalar. Therefore $g \cdot a^{\text{equiv}} + (1-g) \cdot
a^{\text{reason}}$ is equivariant.

\label{sec:ablation}

We compare the full model ($\lambda_{\text{phys}} = 0.5$) with an ablation that
removes all physics losses ($\lambda_{\text{phys}} = 0$) on the gravity system.

The physics loss reduces the instantaneous energy error by $5.2\times$ and the 50-step
drift by $1.4\times$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, Figure~\ref{fig:comparison}). It also
improves MSE by $2.2\times$, acting as a regulariser: by penalising physically
implausible accelerations, the conservation loss constrains the optimisation landscape to
solutions consistent with the underlying dynamics.

The reasoning-only model achieves moderate MSE (Table~\ref{tab:reasoning})---worse than
the full model by $5.7\times$ for spring---but it proves that the interpretable pathway
\emph{can} learn the force law independently. The spring chain topology recall is 0.368
for reasoning-only versus 0.615 for the full model, suggesting that the equivariant
pathway provides useful gradients that improve the reasoning module's interaction-graph
recovery. This is the expressiveness--interpretability tradeoff: the full model is more
accurate but distributes the force across two pathways, while the reasoning-only model is
less accurate but its edge weights directly encode the interaction structure.

\subsection{Interaction-graph recovery}
\label{sec:graph}

For the spring chain, we evaluate topology recall: among the top-$|E_{\text{true}}|$ edges
ranked by $|w_{ij}|$, what fraction are true chain-adjacent pairs? The full model
achieves recall 0.615 (versus 0.50 expected by chance for top-6 of 12 ordered edges),
and the interaction strengths show positive correlation with the true force structure
(strength correlation 0.283 for gravity, indicating partial recovery).

The topology recall and strength correlation are modest, reflecting a fundamental
tradeoff: when the gate blends two pathways, the reasoning module's edge weights capture
only a \emph{partial} view of the true force. Future work can improve interpretability
by explicitly regularising the edge weights against the true pairwise force, or by
designing the gate to preferentially use the reasoning pathway for pairs with high
interaction strength.

\subsection{Generalisation to unseen masses and configurations}
\label{sec:generalization}

We evaluate generalisation on a held-out test set with different random seeds for
initial conditions and masses. Table~\ref{tab:main} shows generalisation MSE is within
$1.5\times$ of test MSE for gravity and \emph{lower} than test MSE for spring and LJ,
demonstrating that PhysRNet learns the functional form of the force law rather than
memorising specific trajectories.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What we have shown.} PhysRNet combines three proven ideas---equivariant message
passing, physics-informed loss regularisation, and interpretable graph-based force
prediction---into a single architecture that is exactly rotation-equivariant, learns
three distinct force laws, generalises to unseen configurations, and reduces conservation
error via physics-informed training. The architecture's exact equivariance is verified to
machine precision ($\leq 3 \times 10^{-12}$), and the per-node gate reveals how the
model allocates responsibility between the symmetric and interpretable pathways.

\textbf{The exact equivariance guarantee.} Unlike empirical equivariance (which is tested
on specific rotations), the architectural guarantee holds for \emph{all} rotations and
\emph{all} inputs simultaneously, by construction. This is possible because every scalar
coefficient in the architecture is a function of rotation-invariant features only, and
every vector is either a scalar $\times$ equivariant vector or an equivariant vector
itself. The machine-precision verification ($6 \times 10^{-14}$ for gravity) confirms
that the implementation matches the guarantee up to floating-point arithmetic.

\textbf{Conservation as a regulariser.} The physics-informed loss improves both
conservation \emph{and} MSE, suggesting that the conservation constraints constrain the
optimisation landscape to physically plausible solutions. This is consistent with the
broader finding that inductive biases improve sample efficiency
\citep{brandstetter2022geometric}, and suggests that conservation laws are particularly
effective inductive biases for physical force-law learning.

\textbf{Interpretable interaction graphs.} The reasoning module's edge weights provide
an explicit, differentiable map from particle states to interaction strengths. The spring
chain topology recall (0.615) and gravity strength correlation (0.283) demonstrate partial
recovery; the full model distributes the force across both pathways, limiting individual
interpretability. This is an honest result: the interaction graph is a harder target than
acceleration prediction, and recovering it from end-to-end training without explicit
supervision is a genuine open problem.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) The experiments are at small scale (3--4 particles, 2D). Scaling
to larger N requires replacing the fully-connected graph with a kNN or
distance-cutoff graph, and GPU training for faster iteration. (ii) The interaction-graph
recovery metrics are modest; explicit supervision on edge weights could improve them.
(iii) The physics module assumes the potential function is known; extending to
\emph{discovering} the potential (as in Hamiltonian neural networks) is future work.

\section*{Reproducibility and broader impact}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All code, committed results, figures, and this paper's source are in the repository
(\texttt{github.com/sehajr-singhs/physrnet}). Every experiment is a self-contained
script with fixed seeds. All experiments run on CPU in under three minutes per system.
Installation: \texttt{pip install -r requirements.txt}.

\paragraph{Data availability.}
All datasets are generated synthetically within the codebase; no external data is
required. The generation scripts are committed and deterministic (fixed seeds).

\paragraph{Code availability.}
The \texttt{physrnet} library is released under the MIT License. All modules,
training loops, evaluation metrics, and figure-generation scripts are in the repository.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
PhysRNet contributes to the growing body of work on physics-aware machine learning.
The exact equivariance guarantee has potential applications in scientific computing,
molecular dynamics, and robotics, where incorrect symmetry treatment can lead to
unphysical predictions. The conservation-constrained training paradigm could improve
long-horizon physical simulations. The interpretable interaction-graph module connects
to the broader interpretability agenda in AI safety.
