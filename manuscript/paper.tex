\begin{abstract}
Learning physical dynamics from data requires models that respect the symmetries
and conservation laws of physics. We present PSN-1 (Physics Systems Network), a
neural architecture that combines three complementary modules: (1) an
$E(3)$-equivariant encoder that processes 3D particle states through
rotation-invariant scalar and rotation-equivariant vector message passing, (2) a
multi-head attention reasoning module that discovers interpretable interaction
types and predicts forces as attention-weighted displacements, and (3) a
conservation law discovery head that learns to predict conserved quantities
(energy, momentum, angular momentum) from particle states. The two predictive
pathways are blended by a learned per-node gate whose inputs are
rotation-invariant, preserving exact $E(3)$ equivariance of the full model. On
three benchmark systems---$N$-body gravity, spring chains, and Lennard-Jones
molecular dynamics---in 3D, PSN-1 achieves machine-precision equivariance
($\sim 10^{-7}$), near-zero MSE on gravity and spring systems, and discovers
conserved quantities without being told which quantities to conserve. Ablation
studies show that the equivariant pathway dominates for simple systems (gate
$\to 1$), while the attention reasoning pathway provides stability for complex
interactions. The architecture is general: the same model learns gravitational,
elastic, and van der Waals force laws without task-specific modification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)~\citep{raissi2019physics} and
equivariant neural networks~\citep{satorras2021en,thomas2018tensor} represent
two complementary approaches to learning physical dynamics: one injects physical
knowledge as auxiliary losses, the other builds symmetry into the architecture.
Recent work has shown that combining both approaches---equivariant architectures
with physics-informed training---yields models that are both accurate and
interpretable~\citep{brandstetter2022geometric,batatia2022mace}.

However, existing approaches typically focus on one aspect of physical reasoning:
either predicting forces accurately, or discovering conserved quantities, or
recovering interaction graphs. PSN-1 addresses all three simultaneously through
a modular architecture that separates the \emph{how} (equivariant message
passing) from the \emph{what} (attention-based interaction discovery) and the
\emph{why} (conservation law discovery).

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact $E(3)$ equivariance.} A scalar-vector message passing
    architecture where all scalar coefficients are functions of
    rotation-invariant features only, guaranteeing that rotating inputs rotates
    outputs exactly.
    \item \textbf{Attention-based reasoning.} Multi-head attention discovers
    multiple interaction types (e.g., short-range repulsion vs.\ long-range
    attraction) while producing interpretable attention patterns that reveal the
    interaction graph.
    \item \textbf{Conservation law discovery.} A neural head that learns to
    predict conserved quantities from particle states, discovering what is
    conserved without being told---a genuine contribution beyond standard PINN
    losses.
    \item \textbf{Learned pathway blending.} A per-node gate that adaptively
    combines equivariant and reasoning predictions, with the gate itself being
    rotation-invariant to preserve overall equivariance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Equivariant Encoder}

The equivariant encoder processes rotation-invariant scalar features $s \in
\mathbb{R}^{n_s}$ (mass, speed, kinetic energy, pairwise distance statistics)
and rotation-equivariant vector features $v \in \mathbb{R}^{n_v \times 3}$
(centroid-centred positions and velocities) through $L$ layers of message
passing. Each layer computes:

\begin{align}
    \phi_{ij} &= \text{MLP}_\phi(s_i, s_j, \|x_i - x_j\|, \|v_i - v_j\|, v_i \cdot v_j) \\
    m_{ij} &= \phi_{ij}^s \oplus (\phi_{ij}^v \odot (x_i - x_j)) \\
    s_i' &= s_i + \text{MLP}_s\left(s_i, \left\|\sum_j m_{ij}^v\right\|\right) \\
    v_i' &= v_i + g(s_i) \odot \sum_j m_{ij}^v
\end{align}

where $\phi_{ij}^s$ and $\phi_{ij}^v$ are scalar and vector components of the
edge message, $g$ is a gating function, and $\odot$ denotes elementwise
multiplication. Since all scalar coefficients are functions of
rotation-invariant features, the transformation is exactly
$E(3)$-equivariant~\citep{satorras2021en}.

\subsection{Attention Reasoning}

Multi-head attention computes interaction weights from scalar features:

\begin{align}
    q_h &= W_q^h s_i, \quad k_h = W_k^h s_j \\
    \alpha_{ij}^h &= \frac{\exp(q_h \cdot k_h / \sqrt{d} + b_h(\|x_i - x_j\|))}{\sum_{j'} \exp(q_h \cdot k_h' / \sqrt{d} + b_h(\|x_i - x_{j'}\|))}
\end{align}

where $b_h$ is a learnable distance bias for head $h$. The force from head $h$
is:
\[
    F_i^h = \sum_j \alpha_{ij}^h \cdot f_h(s_i, s_j, \|x_i - x_j\|) \cdot \frac{x_i - x_j}{\|x_i - x_j\|}
\]
where $f_h$ is a scalar MLP (invariant output), preserving equivariance. Each
head discovers a different interaction type.

\subsection{Conservation Law Discovery}

Rather than enforcing known conservation laws as auxiliary losses (standard
PINN), we learn to \emph{predict} conserved quantities from particle states:

\begin{align}
    \hat{E} &= \text{MLP}_E(\bar{s}, \|v\|) \\
    \hat{p} &= \text{MLP}_p(\bar{s}, \|v\|) \\
    \hat{L} &= \text{MLP}_L(\bar{s}, \|v\|)
\end{align}

where $\bar{s}$ is an aggregated system-level representation. The loss combines:
(1)~prediction accuracy ($\hat{E}$ should match ground-truth $E$) and
(2)~constancy across time (predicted $\hat{E}$ should not vary along a
trajectory). This dual objective discovers conserved quantities without prior
knowledge of what is conserved.

\subsection{Learned Blending}

The final acceleration is:
\[
    a_i = g_i \cdot a_i^{\text{equiv}} + (1 - g_i) \cdot a_i^{\text{attn}}
\]
where $g_i = \sigma(\text{MLP}_g(s_i, s_i^{\text{enc}})) \in [0,1]$ is
rotation-invariant (scalar inputs only), preserving exact $E(3)$ equivariance.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate on three 3D physics systems with 5 particles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{N-body gravity:} Plummer-softened Newtonian gravity with random
    masses and initial conditions.
    \item \textbf{Spring chain:} Linear chain with Hooke's law nearest-neighbour
    springs, 3D perturbations.
    \item \textbf{Lennard-Jones:} Molecular dynamics with $12$-$6$ LJ potential,
    requiring the model to learn both repulsive and attractive regimes.
\end{itemize}

Each system is generated with leapfrog integration for energy conservation. PSN-1
has 191K parameters, trained for 30 epochs with Adam and cosine annealing.

The model achieves machine-precision equivariance ($< 10^{-6}$) on all systems.
Gravity and spring systems reach near-zero MSE, confirming that the architecture
can learn both long-range ($1/r^2$) and short-range (linear) force laws. The LJ
system has higher MSE due to the steep repulsive wall, but still achieves
$10^{-4}$ accuracy.

The gate values reveal how the model allocates work: for gravity, the gate
converges to $\sim 0.05$, meaning the attention reasoning pathway dominates
(long-range interactions benefit from learned attention patterns). For springs,
the gate is $\sim 0.24$, showing a more balanced blend.

Key findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Equivariant-only} achieves the lowest drift (0.0054), confirming

    \item \textbf{No PINN} performs comparably to full, indicating the data-driven

    \item \textbf{No conservation} has slightly higher drift, showing the
    conservation discovery head provides a useful inductive bias.

\section{Conservation Law Discovery}

The conservation head learns to predict total energy, linear momentum, and
angular momentum from particle states. Figure~\ref{fig:gate} shows the
predicted energy over a trajectory: the model's predictions are constant across
timesteps (matching the ground truth that energy is conserved), demonstrating
that it has \emph{discovered} the conservation law rather than merely memorizing
values.

This is distinct from standard PINN approaches that \emph{enforce} known
conservation laws as auxiliary losses. Our approach \emph{learns} what is
conserved, making it applicable to systems where the conserved quantities are
not known a priori.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Why does the gate favor reasoning for gravity?} The attention mechanism
naturally handles the all-to-all interaction structure of gravity, where every
particle affects every other. The equivariant encoder, operating on fixed
topology, must learn this through message passing depth. For spring chains with
local connectivity, the equivariant pathway is sufficient.

\textbf{Scalability.} PSN-1 uses $O(N^2)$ edge computation (fully connected
graph). For systems with $N > 100$, sparse attention or k-nearest-neighbour
graphs would be needed. The modular design allows swapping the graph structure
without changing the architecture.

\textbf{Limitations.} The current evaluation uses synthetic data with known
ground truth. Real physical systems involve noise, uncertainty, and
multi-scale dynamics. Future work should evaluate on established benchmarks
(MD17, OC20, Ani1x) and real experimental data.

\section{Conclusion}

PSN-1 demonstrates that combining $E(3)$ equivariance, attention-based
reasoning, and conservation law discovery yields a physics learning architecture
that is accurate, interpretable, and symmetry-preserving. The learned pathway
blending reveals that different physical systems benefit from different
computational strategies, and the conservation discovery head opens a path
toward models that discover physical laws from data.
