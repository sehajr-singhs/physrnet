\begin{abstract}
We present PhysRNet, a three-module architecture for learning physical force laws
from trajectory data. The architecture combines (i)~an E(2)-equivariant encoder with
exact rotation-equivariance by construction (error $\leq 3 \times 10^{-12}$),
(ii)~physics-informed conservation losses (energy, momentum, angular momentum) that
reduce long-horizon drift by 30\%, and (iii)~a graph-based reasoning module that
predicts interaction strengths as learned edge weights. A per-node gate blends the
two predictive pathways while preserving exact equivariance. PhysRNet learns three
conservative force laws---Plummer gravity, Hooke springs, Lennard-Jones---from
synthetic trajectories alone, generalises to unseen masses and configurations, and
runs on CPU in under three minutes per system.
\end{abstract}

equivariant neural networks, physics-informed networks, graph neural networks,

\section{Introduction}

Learning physical force laws from trajectory data requires correct symmetry structure
and conservation constraints. We present \textbf{PhysRNet}, combining three proven
ideas:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Exact E(2)-equivariance.} Every scalar coefficient in the architecture
        is a function of rotation-invariant features only; every vector is a scalar
        $\times$ equivariant vector. The full model is exactly equivariant, verified to
        machine precision.
  \item \textbf{Conservation constraints.} Auxiliary losses penalise violations of
        energy, linear, and angular momentum conservation.
  \item \textbf{Interpretable interaction graph.} The reasoning module predicts signed
        edge weights $w_{ij}$ such that $\mathbf{a}_i = \sum_j w_{ij}\hat{u}_{ij}$.
\end{enumerate}

A per-node learned gate $g_i \in [0,1]$ blends the equivariant and reasoning pathways
while preserving exact equivariance.

\section{Architecture}

\subsection{Node features}
Particle states $(\mathbf{r}_i, \mathbf{v}_i, m_i)$ are encoded as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Invariant scalars:} $\mathbf{s}_i = [m_i, |\mathbf{v}_i|,
        \tfrac{1}{2}m_i|\mathbf{v}_i|^2, |\mathbf{r}_i - \bar{\mathbf{r}}|] \in
        \mathbb{R}^4$.
  \item \textbf{Equivariant vectors:} centroid-centred position and velocity, padded
        to 3D, as 2 vector channels.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Equivariant encoder}
$L=2$ rounds of message passing over a fully-connected graph. Edge features are
rotation-invariant ($\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{s}_j$, distance norms, dot products).
Scalar messages are MLPs of invariant features. Vector messages are $\sigma(\text{MLP})
\times (\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_j)$---scalar gate $\times$ equivariant carrier.
Node updates multiply aggregated vector messages by invariant scalar gates.

\subsection{Equivariant acceleration head}
From the encoder's equivariant vector features, a linear head with invariant scalar
coefficients produces per-node acceleration:
$\mathbf{a}_i^{\text{equiv}} = \sum_c c_i \cdot \mathbf{v}_{i,c}^{\text{enc}}|_{\text{2D}}$.

\subsection{Reasoning GNN}
The reasoning module computes per-pair interaction weights:
$w_{ij} = \text{MLP}([\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{s}_j, d_{ij}, |\Delta\mathbf{v}|])$,
and predicts acceleration as $\mathbf{a}_i^{\text{reason}} = \sum_j w_{ij}\hat{u}_{ij}$.
The learned edge weights $w_{ij}$ are the recovered interaction graph.

\subsection{Physics residual}
Conservation-law losses from semi-implicit Euler prediction:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} = |E_{\text{pred}} - E_{\text{true}}|^2 +
  0.5|\mathbf{p}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{true}}|^2 +
  0.5|\mathbf{L}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{L}_{\text{true}}|^2
\end{equation}

\subsection{Training}
Loss: $\mathcal{L} = \|a_{\text{pred}} - a_{\text{true}}\|^2 +
\lambda_{\text{phys}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}$, $\lambda_{\text{phys}}=0.5$.
Adam ($2\times 10^{-3}$), cosine annealing, batch 512, 60 epochs.

\textbf{Force-law learning.} PhysRNet learns all three force laws from trajectories
alone (Table~\ref{tab:results}). Gravity achieves the lowest MSE ($4.1\times10^{-4}$);
spring and LJ converge to $\sim 10^{-2}$ within 60 epochs.

\textbf{Exact equivariance.} The rotation error $\|R \cdot a(x) - a(Rx)\|^2$ is at
machine precision ($\leq 3 \times 10^{-12}$) for all systems and all model variants.
This is an architectural guarantee, not an empirical property.

\textbf{PINN ablation.} On gravity, removing the physics loss increases MSE by
$\times 2.2$ and energy drift by $\times 1.4$, confirming that conservation constraints
act as effective regularisers.

\textbf{Reasoning-only.} Freezing the gate ($g=0$) shows the reasoning module alone
achieves moderate MSE (spring: $0.19$ versus $0.034$ for the full model), proving
the interpretable pathway learns the force law independently.

\section{Conclusion}

PhysRNet combines exact symmetry, conservation, and interpretability into a single
architecture for physical force-law learning. The exact equivariance guarantee is
verified to machine precision, the conservation loss reduces drift by 30\%, and the
reasoning module recovers interaction strengths as explicit edge weights. All results
are CPU-reproducible from committed code.
